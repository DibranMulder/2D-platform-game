# Playable Lineage art-direction handoff

## Purpose and terminology

This brief gives concept artists a common starting point for the eight playable
peoples. They are **Lineages**, not Combat Classes: a Lineage defines a Hero's
body, cultural origin, visual heritage, and Homeland. Combat Class will later
define learned professions and fighting roles independently.

The eight-Lineage roster, Allegiances, and Homelands are confirmed. All unique
Lineage names, palettes, anatomy details, costumes, emblems, and architecture
below remain working art direction until reviewed.

## Shared visual direction

Aim for original storybook high fantasy with strong faction identity, majestic
natural realms, and readable side-scrolling silhouettes. The references are a
tone target—not a license to reproduce characters, costumes, names, symbols,
creatures, or architecture from *Age of Wonders*, *Narnia*, *The Lord of the
Rings*, *MapleStory*, or any other existing property.

The selected rendering target is **cinematic storybook anime**, represented by
[cinematic-storybook-anime-v1.png](../../art-source/style-references/cinematic-storybook-anime-v1.png).
Use graceful proportions around four to five heads tall, refined anime facial
design, fine ink-like contours, controlled cel shading, and rich
watercolor-and-gouache texture. Characters, equipment, creatures, and scenery
must look as if they came from one art team. Preserve gameplay readability by
keeping the active plane and character contours crisper and higher-contrast than
the softly painted middle and distant layers.

- Every Lineage must read from silhouette at normal gameplay scale.
- Body shape, clothing shape, material language, and movement should all echo
  the Homeland without making every citizen a soldier.
- Light and Dark are provisional cosmic-political Allegiances, not measures of
  beauty, intelligence, or personal morality. Dark Lineages must not be made
  grotesque merely to communicate their Allegiance.
- Each Lineage needs varied ages, genders, builds, professions, and social
  roles while preserving a recognizable visual grammar.
- Environment concepts must include traversable platforms, vertical routes,
  foreground cover, distant landmarks, and a safe settlement silhouette.

### Carried gameplay equipment

- Every playable-Lineage character exploration must visibly carry or ready at
  least one player-equippable weapon, combat staff, focus, or shield. Civilian
  professions may still be communicated through clothing and secondary gear,
  but baskets, surveying tools, lanterns, farming tools, and similar props must
  not replace the character's gameplay equipment.
- Equipment must use the shared item and weapon vocabulary rather than becoming
  biologically or culturally locked to a Lineage. A Lineage may influence its
  materials and ornament, not who is allowed to equip it.
- Record the item ID or provisional weapon-family ID beside every selected
  concept. Do not canonize an invented weapon or staff from an image alone.
- Approved Weapon Families are Sword, Axe, Spear, Polearm, Crossbow, Bow, Wand,
  Staff, Dagger, and Blunt. Use the Grip and Shield compatibility rules in the
  equipment design: any One-Handed Weapon may accompany a Shield; Two-Handed
  Weapons occupy both hands. Do not imply dual wielding.
- Across each ten-character Lineage sheet, show a deliberate mix of melee,
  ranged, and mystic equipment; One-Handed Weapon and Shield pairings; and
  Two-Handed Weapons. Weapon choice must not make a Lineage look restricted to
  one Combat Class.
- The currently implemented prototype examples are the `rusty_sword`
  One-Handed Sword and `driftwood_buckler` Shield. Other approved families
  remain gameplay vocabulary rather than named items until their definitions
  and combat behavior are added.

## Light Allegiance

### Tidekin — The Sea

**Core fantasy:** Primarily frog-like amphibious navigators, divers, traders,
and reef guardians. Their strength is elasticity, balance, and an intimate
understanding of tides—not generic fish magic.

**Silhouette and movement:** Rounded heads, prominent eyes, strong thighs,
webbed hands and feet, compact torsos, and optional fin or throat-fan variation.
Use low elastic anticipation, powerful leaps, soft landings, and swimming-like
weapon arcs. Avoid turning them into comic frog mascots or one uniform body type.

**Materials and palette:** Shell, polished coral, pearl, woven kelp, sea glass,
waterproof hide, and verdigris bronze. Lead with deep teal, sea green, foam
cream, and indigo; use coral orange or pearl-pink as warm accents. Favor rings,
ripples, droplets, and overlapping scale-like plates.

**Terrain cues:** Tidal terraces, mangrove roots, coral arches, flooded halls,
bioluminescent pools, wave-worn shrines, and structures that change function as
the water rises. Platforms should feel grown, moored, or carved by water.

### Humans — The Open Lands

**Core fantasy:** Adaptable road builders, farmers, explorers, wardens, and
courtly houses who connect the broad terrestrial kingdoms. They should provide
the setting's grounded storybook anchor without becoming the visually generic
default option.

**Silhouette and movement:** Clear upright proportions with strong variation in
height, age, and build. Use layered cloaks, practical belts, readable heraldic
shapes, and balanced equipment. Movement is trained and economical rather than
supernatural.

**Materials and palette:** Linen, wool, oak, hammered brass, weathered steel,
leather, and glazed pottery. Lead with royal blue, warm ivory, field green, and
oxblood; use muted gold for rank and ceremony. Develop an original knot, braid,
or road motif rather than familiar real-world heraldry.

**Terrain cues:** Rolling meadows, wind-bent grass, orchards, old roads, chalk
cliffs, river crossings, hill forts, market towns, and weathered keeps. Platforms
can be bridges, rooftops, ruined walls, giant roots, and terraced farmland.

### Grove Centaurs — The Elder Forests

**Core fantasy:** Swift forest guardians, pathfinders, herbalists, and oral
historians bound to ancient groves. Their culture should feel mobile and
ecologically integrated rather than like humans placed onto horse bodies.

**Silhouette and movement:** A long, low equine base beneath a tall torso creates
the roster's clearest horizontal silhouette. Explore horse-body diversity,
manes, tail dress, body paint, and harness systems that do not imitate human
saddles. Animation should emphasize momentum, controlled rearing, sweeping
turns, and confident traversal under a dense canopy.

**Materials and palette:** Living wood, bark lamellae, woven reeds, leaf fiber,
soft leather, bronze, amber, and carved seed pods. Lead with moss, fern, bark
brown, and warm cream; accent with amber and luminous spring green. Use branching
lines, leaf veins, antler-like forks, and concentric growth rings.

**Terrain cues:** Root bridges, hollow elder trees, canopy roads, luminous
clearings, hanging gardens, fern valleys, and settlements built around—not
through—living trunks. Platform spacing must be tested against the longer body.

### Aeralith — The Sky Reaches

**Core fantasy:** Wind-shaped highlanders, couriers, astronomers, and navigators
who travel between elevated realms. Their magic should feel like lift, pressure,
sound, and weather rather than generic angelic holiness.

**Silhouette and movement:** Tall, light frames, elevated hair or crests, long
split mantles, and translucent wind vanes or gliding membranes. Full feathered
wings are not assumed; the first concept pass should compare winged, gliding,
and purely air-touched anatomies. Movement uses long hang time, poised landings,
and fabric that responds constantly to invisible currents.

**Materials and palette:** Layered silk, lacquered light wood, brass, pale stone,
crystal, and taut membrane. Lead with cloud white, azure, blue-grey, and soft
lavender; accent with sunrise gold. Use spirals, streamlines, kites, wind harps,
and open arches. Avoid default angels, bird people, or slender elves with wings.

**Terrain cues:** Floating mesas, cloud forests, rope bridges, sail lifts,
observatories, storm shelters, wind-carved spires, and hanging bells. Platforms
should suggest updrafts, suspension, and dangerous open air.

## Dark Allegiance

### Crag Trolls — The Broken Mountains

**Core fantasy:** Monumental mountain clans shaped by thin air, avalanches,
quarries, and violent storms. They are patient builders and survivalists as well
as formidable warriors.

**Silhouette and movement:** Top-heavy frames, long arms, broad hands, low necks,
and skin planes that suggest weathered rock without literally making every Troll
stone. Use slow anticipation followed by decisive weight and impact. Keep faces
capable of intelligence, tenderness, and age; avoid the stupid brute stereotype.

**Materials and palette:** Basalt, slate, raw iron, horn, thick hide, rope, and
lichen-dyed cloth. Lead with granite blue, charcoal, umber, and storm grey;
accent with rust orange and pale lichen. Favor wedges, stacked slabs, fault lines,
and hammered metal bands.

**Terrain cues:** Sheer cliffs, quarry fortresses, avalanche chutes, rope hoists,
storm forges, narrow passes, and monumental stairs. Platforms should look split,
stacked, anchored, and capable of bearing immense weight.

### Deep Goblins — The Underdeep

**Core fantasy:** Ingenious tunnel societies of miners, machinists, mushroom
cultivators, scouts, and traders. Their technology is adapted to scarce space,
air, light, and fuel—not random scrap for comedy.

**Silhouette and movement:** Compact, forward-leaning bodies, large sensory
features, layered tool belts, protective lenses, and backpacks that clearly show
function. Movement is quick, low, alert, and route-efficient. Faces should convey
calculation and community rather than constant mischief.

**Materials and palette:** Sooted iron, copper, leather, fungal fiber, glass,
ceramic pipe, crystal, and carefully reused surface goods. Lead with soot black,
copper, umber, and fungus violet; accent with chartreuse or cyan bioluminescence.
Use gears sparingly alongside diamonds, tunnel ribs, rivets, rails, and mycelial
networks.

**Terrain cues:** Fungal forests, crystal seams, pumps, chain lifts, railways,
ventilation towers, underground canals, and dense stacked settlements. Platforms
should look excavated, braced, suspended, or machine-driven.

### Sunscour — The Ember Desert

**Core fantasy:** A disciplined desert people whose society is shaped by heat,
distance, water stewardship, mirages, and long fortified routes. **Sunscour** is
the working people-name; the **Sunscour Legion** should be treated as one military
organization within that culture, not as the biological Lineage itself.

**Silhouette and movement:** Lean, upright figures protected by layered,
asymmetrical wraps, articulated scale pieces, sand veils, and compact travel
gear. Their movement is measured, coordinated, and economical in the heat, with
short bursts of speed. Do not reproduce Haradrim silhouettes or reduce the
culture to turbans, curved swords, and exoticized menace.

**Materials and palette:** Sun-bleached linen, dark cooling cloth, blackened
bronze, glazed ceramic, leather, salt crystal, and colored glass. Lead with sand,
indigo, ember red, and charcoal; accent with turquoise or bright brass. Use the
split sun, horizon line, mirage wave, geometric shade screens, and water tallies
as starting motifs developed into an original visual language.

**Terrain cues:** Dunes, basalt canyons, salt flats, shaded citadels, cisterns,
caravan roads, solar furnaces, and monumental aqueducts. Platforms should use
cloth shade, eroded rock, scaffolds, bridges, and heat-distorted ruins.

### Rimeborn — The Ice Lands

**Core fantasy:** Cold-adapted clans of hunters, navigators, craftspeople, and
keepers of warmth who survive the long night beyond the glaciers. Their identity
comes from communal endurance and precise preparation, not simply ice magic.

**Silhouette and movement:** Layered, insulated figures with strong boots,
protected extremities, compact packs, and visible systems for rope, shelter, and
heat. Explore varied human-like and more fantastical anatomy before canonizing
skin color or facial traits. Movement is traction-conscious and deliberate,
followed by sudden committed bursts.

**Materials and palette:** Layered fur, felt, bone, dark iron, sealable leather,
ice glass, and luminous mineral pigment. Lead with midnight navy, glacial blue,
moon white, and black rock; accent with aurora green or magenta and hearth amber.
Use hexagonal fracture, spear points, braided cord, constellations, and enclosed
circles of warmth. Avoid copying *Age of Wonders* Frostlings or relying only on
generic Viking imagery.

**Terrain cues:** Ice shelves, frozen forests, black volcanic ridges, thermal
springs, aurora halls, snow-buried ruins, and mobile windbreak settlements.
Platforms should show fracture risk, rope safety lines, ice caves, and protected
sources of heat.

## First concept-art delivery

For each Lineage, provide:

1. Ten small black silhouette explorations, including different builds and
   non-combat professions; every figure must also carry visible gameplay
   equipment under the shared equipment rule.
2. One front/side character sheet with palette and material callouts.
3. Run, jump, basic attack, and Guard key poses at intended gameplay scale.
4. One settlement-and-terrain keyframe designed for a 2D side-scrolling route.
5. One original emblem sheet and a page of shape-language components.
6. A short list of visual similarities noticed with existing franchises so
   those similarities can be removed before production art begins.

Do not proceed to polished production sprites until silhouettes for all eight
Lineages are compared together in one lineup and the Aeralith anatomy and
Sunscour people-name have been reviewed.

## Open decisions for art review

- Are Tidekin strictly amphibian, or may some families show reptilian traits?
- Do Aeralith have wings, gliding anatomy, or only supernatural control of air?
- Are Sunscour a distinct non-human people, a Human culture, or an Allegiance
  spanning multiple Lineages?
- How visibly non-human are Rimeborn beneath their cold-weather equipment?
- Which symbols represent Allegiance, and which belong only to a Lineage,
  nation, faith, guild, or military organization?
