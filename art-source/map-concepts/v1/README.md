Each board shows the same two navigation levels:

- **World Map:** large clickable Regions only.
- **Region Map:** discovered Maps connected by physical Portal routes, including
  a five-to-ten-Map Stronghold cluster and a Dungeon tunnel at least ten depths
  deep.

The boards intentionally avoid designing individual side-scrolling Maps. Their
purpose is to select the navigation metaphor and visual theme before producing
hundreds of content assets.

## Variants

### A — Living Illustrated Atlas

`variant-a-living-illustrated-atlas.png`

A direct full-screen atlas. Natural borders and landmark emblems define the
World Map; a geographic painting with roads and landmark nodes defines the
Region Map. This is the most immediate and least metaphor-heavy option.

### B — Enchanted Chronicle

`variant-b-enchanted-chronicle.png`

An illuminated open book. Regions become painted chapters; Stronghold and
Dungeon graphs expand as page insets. This has the strongest storybook identity
and the clearest place for lore, discovery notes, and expandable clusters.

### C — Wayfinder Table

`variant-c-wayfinder-table.png`

A layered magical cartographer's table. Selecting a Region lifts a relief layer
over the World Map; Portal routes glow through terrain and Dungeon depths descend
through a cutaway. This communicates hierarchy and depth most dramatically.

## Shared prompt constraints

All variants use the approved cinematic storybook-anime reference for fine
ink-like contours, watercolor-and-gouache texture, dark royal-blue framing,
weathered brass, luminous natural magic, and restrained interactive markers.
They must communicate World Map → Region → connected Maps, remain discovery and
navigation views rather than fast travel, conceal Hidden Maps without visible
placeholder nodes, and avoid copied franchise locations, symbols, characters,
or typography.

## Review questions

1. Which metaphor should be canonical: atlas, chronicle, or wayfinder table?
2. Should Stronghold and Dungeon internals expand as insets or replace the
   Region Map temporarily?
3. Is the ten-depth Dungeon chain readable without dominating the Region?
4. Should the chosen design retain geographic placement, explicit Portal graph
   clarity, or a blend of both?
